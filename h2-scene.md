  # c) Science that:
  - Article:
    Lallie, H.S., et al. (2025). Analysing Cyber Attacks and Cyber Security Vulnerabilities in the University Sector. https://www.mdpi.com/2073-431X/14/2/49

    The article systematically investigates security vulnerabilities, threat actors, and risks in the institutional and public sectors. Although the article focuses primarily on cybersecurity and IT infrastructures, there are significant conceptual overlaps with the case of identity theft and system failure described in Example 1 (William Woods):
    The main issue stems from vulnerabilities in authentication and verification processes. The article emphasizes that insufficient control mechanisms and a lack of validation processes make organizations extremely vulnerable to attacks. The case of Matthew Kierans reveals drastic gaps in identity verification by banks, government agencies, and institutions over a period of more than 30 years. Just as digital authentication systems can be compromised by weak protocols, the identity of the real William Woods was permanently and undetected stolen through a series of false documents (IDs, bank cards).
    This leads into the second point: Human factors, trust, and social engineering. In security research, human factors, insufficient awareness, and blind trust in the data provided play a central role in security incidents. In this case, Kierans exploited his prior relationship with Woods and used deliberate manipulation to impersonate another person over the long term. Because authorities and banks placed more trust in the presented proof of identity than in the statements of the real victim, the deception worked for decades.
    Finally, there is a third point of overlap between the article and the example: consequential damages and institutional failure.
    The study discusses the far-reaching operational, legal, and psychological consequences of security and incident management failures for those affected.
    In this case in the example, the complete failure of the oversight authorities led to extreme human and legal consequences (including the wrongful conviction and commitment of the real William Woods to a psychiatric hospital). Only modern forensic methods (such as DNA analysis) brought the truth to light, similar to how digital forensics is used to investigate cyberattacks.
