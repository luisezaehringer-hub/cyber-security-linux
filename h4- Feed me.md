# x) Why would you want to use a feed reader (and RSS/Atom)?
To explain, why using a feed reader is more convenient then just visiting every page, it is mandatory to understand what feed readers do. 
For this, the following source summed it up. "An RSS reader is an app that collects the newest articles, videos and podcast episodes from the websites you choose and shows them in one list. 
Instead of visiting each site to see what is new, you follow it once, and new posts come to you. 
RSS readers are also called feed readers, news readers or RSS aggregators" (cf. feeder, 2026). 
A feed reader will tell you the most important news like in a summary, so you will safe time by not having to browse through every webpage, podcast etc. 
You will get automatic notifications to news, all new content is collected in one place and it can be anonymous, since you dont have to provide personal data for a subscription (cf. ISEC, n.d.). 
These are only three of many other benefits of a feed reader.

## The other benefits can be summarized with the following points:
- time and efficiency: has been described above
- No Algorithms or Noise: Social media and search engines use black-box algorithms to decide what content we see, rank posts by engagement, or inject sponsored ads.
  RSS feeds are purely chronological, we only see what we subscribed to.
- Privacy and Freedom: You maintain full ownership of your reading list and can export or import your subscriptions anytime using standard OPML files
- A Consistent, Distraction-Free Layout: Every website has a different layout, pop-ups, cookie banners, and auto-play videos. Feed readers strip away the clutter, presenting articles in a clean, uniform, and often ad-free text format
- Never Miss Infrequent Updates: If a site only publishes once a month, its likely to forget to check it manually. A reader quietly sits in the background and lights up the moment a new post drops

# y) Supreme court of Finland
- Facts of the case: An individual conducted a port scan on the servers and network of Osuuspankkikeskus-OPK osuuskunta (banking group) to identify  open ports and potentially vulnerable services
- Court decision: The Supreme Court classified straightforward port scanning as attempted unauthorized access (attempted intrusion into a computer system / tietomurron yritys)
- The court’s reasoning: A port scan is no longer merely a harmless preparatory act, but rather marks the actual beginning of the commission of the offense, as it actively seeks out security vulnerabilities in order to gain unauthorized access to protected systems. Furthermore, the scan disrupted network security and necessitated defensive measures
- The public prosecutor's office, the cooperative, and the company filed an appeal with the Court of Appeals. The Court of Appeals found it clear that A had committed the attempted data breach, for which he had to be punished.
- A was granted leave to appeal. In his appeal, A requested that the charges and the claim for damages against him be dismissed. In addition, A requested that the claim for punitive damages be settled in any event.

- analysis and explanation:
  
Initial Situation (Isolated System): By disabling the network interface (ip link set enp0s1 down) and verifying this via ping 8.8.8.8, I ensured that the virtual machine was completely offline and had no connection to the Internet or external networks.
The localhost scan: The scan of the loopback address (127.0.0.1) revealed which services are accessible internally on the computer.
The Daemon Effect: After starting the daemon (nginx), port 80/tcp (open, HTTP) appeared in the nmap table. This proves that a port scan directly reflects which software services are actively listening for requests in the background.

# How can criminals know what services your employer has?
To answer this question, the exercise showed how criminals might operate: Attackers use these scans to find open ports (services) on the network. Each open port (such as port 80 or 22) represents a program that is waiting for requests.
Security risk: If a service is running outdated software with known security vulnerabilities, attackers can exploit them in a targeted manner (as was deemed to constitute unauthorized access or an attempted break-in in the Finnish court ruling in Subtask y).
