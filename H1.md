# b)	Review of the review
1)	Key areas of research
-	Designing tailored models for specific sectors, so the development of new maturity models (such as healthcare, smart grids, cloud computing, and public administration) as well as creating lightweight, accessible models specially adapted for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs).
-	Applying and adapting widely recognized standards to real-world organizational environments to assess current security posture. This means an implementation of established models and frameworks (like ISO/IEC 27001 and so on).
-	Developing quantitative, automated, analytical, or multi-criteria decision-making methods to reduce subjectivity, increase measurement precision, and support continuous maturity monitoring, this indicates an advancement of assessment methodologies.
2)	Whats widely agreed upon?
-	Cyber security is a core business risk, is directly tied to organizational resilience and business continuity, shifting it from an isolated IT issue to a strategic board-level responsibility.
-	A Need for domain-specific adaption, approaches for every solution are insufficient. Effective security maturity assessments must be tailored to specific industries, organizational sizes and operational contexts.
-	Value of maturity models: mutaurity assessments provide a structured evolutionary path for evaluating current capabilities, identifying security gaos, prioritizing improvements as well as progress.
3)	What are the open questions?
-	How to effectively adapt security maturity models to evaluate risks associated with AI, IoT and Industry 4.0 environments. So, in general on how to integrate emerging technologies.
-	Dynamic and automated assessments: How to transition from static self-assessments to continuous, real time and automated security maturity monitoring.
-	How to establish standardized validation frameworks to evaluate the effectiveness, reliability and comparability of newly proposed maturity models across different organizations, the standardization and validation are still open to discuss.
# c)	Threat modeling
Risk 1: A cyber attack got internal data from the company. Clients data are now openly accessible and run through different environments. 
Risk 2: A license is running out and cant get renewed for the company. The company moght need to overthink their whole working method
The company, a tax advisory law firm, is now responsible for bringing the cyberattack (scenario 1) under control and minimizing the damage as much as possible. The crown jewels here are the stolen data, private information about clients, their status, financial situation etc. The bad guys in this scenario are the cyber attackers, through a leak of data they got to their goal, to access the internal data und get information about the clients. The capability can be discribed as an ongoing motivation, since they are capable to access the whole network of clients, get a wide range of personal data and can forward important internal data. The opportunity is to sell this data to an external, third party and to make money with that. This also describes the intent of the hackers, they dont want to get to know the clients, they want to know their needs, so that they can sell these information to others. 
Likelihood x impact = estimated value -> higher likelihood, since law firms are attractive for hackers, 500.000€ as an impact, depending on the comapny size
0,15 x 500.000€ = 75.000€ as an estimated value

For scenario 2, the running out of the license, it can be desriced as follows; the crown juwels are the Software, that the company is using right now. This carries thw whole working traffic, without the software solutions (SaaS Technology for example), the company couldnt work properly. On the other side, the Bad Guy is represented through the the licensor, which wont extend the period of using the license. The Capability of the licensor is given because of his opportunity to decrease the usage of the license/Software. He can stop the support for it withing several days. The opportunity is given, when the law firm messed up the monitoring part behind the license, or if the contract has ended. The intention of this is not because of malicious intent, rather, it seeks to enforce its license terms, impose price increases, or discontinue older software versions. 
0,05 x 100.000€ = 5.000€ as an estimated value
In comparison, risk 1 is the bigger one, since the estimated value of 75.000€ is bigger than the estimated value of risk 2. META: 
-	Mitigate: implementation of Multi-Factor Authentification (MFA) across all the different systems. As well as an Incident Response Plan, for the case of such an event. This could also be mentioned in a SOP (Standard operating procedure) as a rule to apply.

-	Accept: This does also not apply to this specific situation. In case of accepting the scenario, the law firm would loose all their clients, and would have multiple problems because of data loss. This could end in a compliance problem and further more. This is not viable.
